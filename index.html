Clipboard History

A clipboard manager that lives in the menu bar on a Mac and the system tray on Windows. It starts when you sign in and quietly saves every copy, text and images, without being opened; on a Mac the menu bar icon pulses once when a copy is saved. Press ⌃⌘V on a Mac or Ctrl+Alt+H on Windows from any app, type to search, and pick with the arrow keys and Return or ⌘1–9 (Ctrl+1–9). Each item has its own copy button, and Copy All (⇧⌘C, Ctrl+Shift+C) copies every item, or just the search matches, line by line. Pin, delete, pause recording or clear history. The list stays strictly newest first, so picking an old item keeps its place, and secrets from password managers are skipped.

  • RoleProduct design and native build
  • StackSwift, SwiftUI and AppKit on macOS; C# WinForms on .NET 8 on Windows
  • StatusDesktop app for macOS 14+ and Windows 10/11

MacTalk

A walkie-talkie for the Mac menu bar: one-click voice calls to the friends you have approved. Add a friend by email, and once they accept, call them from the friends list or with ⌃1–⌃9 from any app. On their Mac the call slides in at the bottom-right corner with Accept and Decline, and an answered call moves to a small floating window with Mute, Speaker and End Call. Sign in with Google or a one-time email code. Friends-only calling is enforced on the server with Row Level Security and SQL functions, so nobody can ring you without an accepted invitation; audio is encrypted peer to peer, and the microphone stays off until the call is answered.

SipBuddy

A water reminder that only goes away once the camera sees you drinking. At sensible times (working days, weekend hours, lunch and sleep respected) a full-screen reminder opens with the front camera; it counts five sips of you, the owner, and then closes, so it can't be dismissed without a drink. Detection runs entirely on the device, with Apple Vision in the Mac menu bar app and MediaPipe face, hand and cup detection on the web, Android and iPhone; video never leaves it. Today shows the day as a ring and a filling drop, Statistics has week and month charts, a streak and CSV export, and four personalities write the nudges. Material 3 on Android, Apple HIG on iPhone.

Time Machine Earth

An interactive historical atlas from 3000 BCE to today: one map, one timeline. Drag the timeline or press play to watch empires rise and fall across 48 documented boundary snapshots, with a short narration of what changed at each one. Borders are never interpolated: the map always shows the latest snapshot at or before the chosen year and says which one. Tap a territory for its dates, area and what came before and after, search with questions like “Who ruled Egypt in 1000 BCE?”, follow 53 curated civilizations on the same timeline, compare two years side by side or with a swipe, and learn with quizzes generated from the boundary data and a daily challenge. Every record cites its source, and known conflicts between datasets are listed in the app.

  • RoleProduct design and front-end build
  • StackReact, TypeScript, Vite, MapLibre GL, Turf, Zustand; Capacitor and a SwiftUI shell

Screen Break

Eye-rest and movement reminders that work around your day, not against it. Screen Break reminds people who work at a screen to rest their eyes (the 20-20-20 rule), move, check their posture and drink water, then gets out of the way. Every reminder has a fixed due time, reminders that fall close together combine into one break, focus mode holds everything except eye rest, and sleep, lunch and out-of-hours restart the timers instead of firing a backlog. Persona3D

Photos of a person in, a rigged and animated 3D character out. A guided six-step wizard takes photos from up to six angles and checks each one in the browser for resolution, lighting, blur and a visible face, then runs a staged job (geometry, textures, rig, animations) that survives a refresh and can be retried from the stage that failed. The studio has a 3D viewer with camera and lighting presets, 26 animation clips with sequences and a prompt-to-motion planner, customisation, and GLB, OBJ, PNG and MP4 export. Privacy is designed in: consent before upload, private storage behind expiring links, per-user rate limits, deletion and retention controls, and a guard that refuses photos on a shared public deployment. The built-in generator makes a parametric character, not a likeness; Meshy is wired in for real reconstruction.

  • RoleProduct design and full-stack build
  • StackNext.js, React, TypeScript, React Three Fiber and three.js, Zustand, Zod, Supabase (optional), Vitest; Capacitor and a SwiftUI shell

Daybook

A personal dashboard for tasks, goals, habits and notes, with an AI assistant that asks before it writes. Tasks with list, board, calendar and priority views, goals with milestones, reminders, a calendar, habits with streaks, notes and an encrypted personal-information vault share one workspace, and Overview shows today at a glance. The assistant (Claude, server side only) reads a compact snapshot of your data, and anything it wants to change comes back as a card you confirm or edit first; AI calls are rate-limited per user and per server. The public version runs in demo mode: each visitor gets a private sample account for a fictional user, held in an in-memory Postgres and reset after a while.
